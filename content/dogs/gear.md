##  Flat Collar
Flat collars are the most common and most useless of the collars. They look nice, but put all the pressure on the throat when making corrections. I still use them on occasion, the best thing I like it for is with tracking. When attached for tracking I let the long lead go between her legs so the pressure is put on the back of the neck instead of the throat. I make no aversive corrections on the track; however, if she pulls into the lead with the flat collar on in this fashion, it will pull her head in the direction of the scent.

## Slip Collar
I love the slip collar. I use this one my wife made all the time. It doesn't provide the nice snappiness corrections of a pinch collar, but it looks nicer and does fine with continuous pressure, making it good for negative reinforcement. This particular one also doubles as an 18" slip lead. You'll notice her tags on this one because I consider this her primary collar.

## Pinch/Prong Collar
The Infamous **PRONG** collar. It gets a bad rap because it can be used for positive punishment. This might be necessary sometimes. We can have a conversation of conditioning a punishment marker in a different video. Today we are just talking about the general use cases for it. Let's go over some important bits here:

| Subject | Notes | 
| -- | -- |
| Sizing | it's better to go too tight over too loose. We want the collar to be able to rotate on the dog; however, if it's too loose we risk reducing the surface area of the collar during a correction. This can potentially injure your pup. Also, if you have 2 dogs running around with collars like this, if they are too loose and there's a fight, you run the risk of the collars getting tangled. Ensure the collar can rotate, but the collar makes full contact all around the neck when the martingale is pulled tight.
| Corrections  | We can use this for momentary or constant pressure when applying corrections. We can make corrections very light and escalate as needed  |

## Agitation Collar
The agitation collar looks a lot like a large flat collar; however, it is more than that. The collar does more than just increase the surface area applied to the dogs throat, it also provides cushioning for the dog to push into. This brings our risk of damaging our dogs throat to nearly zero. The reason for all this is because it's designed for the dog to pull against. You see it a lot in the protection clubs where they use it to bring a dog's drive up. I use it much the same way, but it's not just used for protection. We can use this to create drive for any task.

## E-collar
E-collars are the best tool for negative reinforcement ever. I used to think of these as just a form of punishment that was best used when the dog thought of it as a superstitious super power. However, after working both superstitious and negative reinforcement styles of use, I have decided that negative reinforcement is both easier and superior. Typical use of this collar is between 10-15 with no distractions, 20-25 with a mildly distracting environment, 30-40 for punishment. The collar goes to 100, but I've never seen a reason to exceed 40.

## Harness

In general, the harness is interchangeable with the agitation collar. I use the harness with Griz during Trailing sessions, and we use the collar for Tracking Sessions. The only benefit I get from using the harness over the collar is the classically conditioned response out of Griz. When she puts the harness on, she knows she can go faster and we are not concerned with every footstep. When we use a collar for tracking, she knows there is going to be small articles and food to find on the track, so she goes slower.

## Treats
I don't think this really needs to be here; however, I use different treats for different reasons.

| Treat description | Treat Reason |
| -- | -- |
| Cheap, but healthy, kibble or freeze-dried food | Used most of the time. How our dog feels about the taste matters less than how we give the treat. |
| Bright red/orange and smelly | I use this as a treat for on-track training. I want it to be red or orange so I can see it, but the dog can not. I want it to have some smell in it so the dog notices it in the footstep. |
| Cheese/hotdog Highly tasty treat | This is used for when I need heavy food motivation out of the dog, but I need her to stay flat/calm. That means I'm not playing any chase games or adding frustration with these, generally speaking. |
