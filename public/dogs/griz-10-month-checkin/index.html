As Griz hits 10 months, I wanted to put a few videos together to show how she’s doing.

Working with Kids

Griz being a high energy working line German Shepherd, means she can be hard to handle. Cassius being a 5-year-old child is going to have a hard time managing a dog like her without some training for the both of them. Once a day Cass works with either Griz or Denali to perform some interaction with his dogs. The leash skills are the hardest for a little guy to handle, so when we work on that, I have him choke up on the lead near the collar, we use a pinch or slip collar, and I take the tail end. I give him a chance to administer corrections; however, I use the tail of the lead to give corrections that he misses. For play, Griz has been generalized on long distance arounds, so, that one is pretty easy for him to play fetch with her. Then we work on classical conditioning together as well. He is still deciding on his reward marker, and I’m not too concerned if he changes it too often, because he’s a kid… And also, our dogs are smart enough to pick up on the other classically conditioned signs. If I give Cass food and ask him to work on his reward marker, the dogs are already tuned in because they’ve been through this before.

Trailing

We are still doing tracking, and it’s not really recommended to do both tracking and trailing; however, I’m gonna keep it up anyways. The difference is when I show up to do trailing with search and rescue personnel, they use longer distances with higher-value rewards at the end. Having Griz check every footstep is exhausting, so I don’t enforce it. When I set up tracking, however, we do shorter distances and use smaller rewards. Reactivity

When Griz was in her fear period 4-5 months of age she was bitten by a couple of dogs. This caused some constant fear based reactivity towards off leash dogs. This video is boring because we are catching it early as one should, but I’m putting it here anyways.

The general idea with this type of reactivity is to bring her to the edge of her comfort zone but not cross it. Initially I just wanted to have her in the trees where she could lie down, chill, and watch dogs walk by. Then we graduated to walking the trail with the other dogs. This proved to be an issue; however, I was able to get a lot of progress by adding in some obedience. When she is focusing on me for a heel, she is less worried about the rest of the world, so I use that.
